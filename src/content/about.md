This is where you should describe who you are, what you do, what motivates you,
and what you care about. There are numerous ways to introduce yourself but the
point of any introduction is to provide insight into who *you* in particular, are.
Do you like gardens and rain, on a slightly overcast day with spring shoots jutting
through the undergrowth, the smell of spice and baking filling the air, as you look
out the window with your hot beverage of choice. Or are you a night owl, nestled
deep within your blankets, looking out across the starry landscape of the sky,
charting stars and the cosmos, as you feel the enormity of the universe press upon you.
Whatever the case may be, the point of the introduction is to provide the reader a
glimpse of your inner working, and why they should continue reading your particular
thoughts and experiences, rather than the other present and past stories of humanity
that are freely available to them. Do not get disheartened by the prospects of writing
a good introduction! The only way of writing a good introduction is by stealing,
iterating and listening to feedback of people you care about and respect. It is a
long journey towards written work that inspires but I feel it may be the most rewarding.

## Research interests

Since this is an academic inclined personal website, you should expand upon your
research interests. If you were given a million dollars (or equivalent sum to not
worry about living expenses) what would you feel your time with? What questions
would you want to solve? I highly recommend expanding upon the sorts of problems
you have previously solved and open questions or conjectures that you are working
towards that you wouldn't mind being scooped on! It's important to make it feel like
your research interests construct a cohesive narrative in the reader rather than a
loose jumbled mess of ideas. Possibly tell a contrived apocryphal story that ties
the interests together (just add a disclaimer at the end!). 

Say what your research is about in plain words, then link to the
[Research](/research) page for the papers themselves.

## Contact

Set the boundaries of contact with people of all different walks of life. I feel that
Tom Scott in particular has an [excellent website](https://www.tomscott.com/contact/)
if you want a reference of how to word and deal with solicitation. I would recommend
keeping your contact details on a publicly available website separate to more important
communication channels, unless you are willing to sign up for the task of sorting
through all the unnecessary spam you will collect. Possibly a good purpose for one
shot classifies in the near future!
